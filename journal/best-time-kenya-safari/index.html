When to Go: Kenya's Safari Seasons Decoded

February 2025 5 min read

There is no single "best" month for a Kenya safari — there is only the best month for you. Kenya rewards visitors year-round, but each season unlocks a genuinely different country: different light, different crowds, different wildlife behaviour, and different prices. Here is how the seasons actually break down.

Dry Season (June to October): Peak Wildlife Viewing

This is Kenya's most popular safari season, and for good reason. Vegetation thins out, forcing animals to congregate around rivers and waterholes, which makes game viewing exceptional across nearly every park. From July onward, this window also overlaps with the Great Migration's arrival in the Maasai Mara, when the famous river crossings take place.

Expect the driest, most reliable weather — clear skies, cool mornings, warm afternoons — but also the highest prices and the busiest camps, particularly in the Mara during migration season.

Short Rains (November): The Quiet Shoulder Season

November brings brief, usually afternoon showers that green the landscape without disrupting most safari activity. This shoulder season offers a compelling middle ground: newly lush scenery, thinner crowds, and often lower rates, while wildlife viewing remains strong across most parks.

Dry, Warm Season (January to March): Kilimanjaro at Its Clearest

This period is a personal favourite for Amboseli specifically — the short rains have passed, the air is clear, and Mount Kilimanjaro reveals itself above the plains with a clarity that is much rarer during the hazier mid-year months. It is also calving season on the Serengeti's southern plains just across the border, drawing predators in large numbers. Warm, dry, and markedly quieter than peak season.

Long Rains (April to May): Kenya's Green Season

The long rains bring Kenya's most dramatic transformation — dusty plains turn vividly green, migratory birds arrive in numbers, and newborn wildlife is everywhere. Some camps close for maintenance during this period and certain roads become difficult, but for travellers prioritising value, solitude, and a completely different, verdant Kenya, this is an underrated window. Rates typically drop significantly.

The "worst" time to visit Kenya, weather-wise, is often the best time to have a park entirely to yourself.

Choosing Your Season by Destination

  • For Great Migration river crossings: July through October, in the Maasai Mara.
  • For the clearest Kilimanjaro views: January to March, in Amboseli.
  • For value and solitude: April, May, or November, almost anywhere.
  • For beach weather on the coast: December to March or July to September tend to be driest in Diani and Lamu.

There Is No Wrong Time

Every season in Kenya has genuine wildlife rewards — this is one of the few safari destinations in the world where "off-season" does not mean disappointing. The right time for your trip depends on what you are chasing: the drama of a river crossing, the clarity of a mountain view, or simply having a vast wilderness feel like it belongs to you alone.
