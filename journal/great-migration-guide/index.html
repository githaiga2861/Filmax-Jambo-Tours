The Great Migration: Everything You Need to Know Before You Go

March 2025 8 min read

Two million wildebeest. Hundreds of thousands of zebra and gazelle. One river, crossed again and again, in one of the last truly great movements of wild animals left on Earth. The Great Migration is not a single event but a year-round cycle — and understanding its rhythm is the difference between a good safari and the trip of a lifetime.

What Is the Great Migration?

Each year, roughly two million wildebeest, accompanied by zebra and various gazelle species, follow an ancient, roughly circular route between the Serengeti in Tanzania and the Maasai Mara in Kenya, chasing the rains and the fresh grass that follows them. It is not a single crossing on a single day — it is a continuous, months-long movement, with the famous river crossings representing just its most dramatic chapters.

It is not staged, not scheduled, and never guaranteed on any single day — which is exactly what makes witnessing it feel like such a privilege.

When Does the Migration Reach the Maasai Mara?

The herds typically arrive in Kenya's Maasai Mara ecosystem between July and October, having crossed north from Tanzania's Serengeti. This is when the famous Mara River crossings happen — herds building in tension along the riverbank before committing to the crossing, fighting currents, crocodiles, and steep banks to reach fresh grazing on the other side.

By November, the rains begin pulling the herds back south into Tanzania, where they will calve on the short-grass plains around February and March before beginning the cycle again.

Where to Position Yourself for a Crossing

Crossings can happen at several points along the Mara River, and no guide — however experienced — can promise a crossing on any given day. What experienced safari operators can do is read the signs: herds massing on one bank, agitation building, a lead animal testing the water. Camps and lodges positioned along the river, or those with dedicated spotter networks, give you the best chance of being in the right place when it happens.

  • Riverside camps in the Mara Triangle and along the Talek and Mara rivers offer the fastest access to live crossing points.
  • Private conservancies bordering the main reserve offer a quieter, less crowded vantage, often with better vehicle positioning.
  • A hot air balloon safari at dawn gives a completely different perspective — the migration as a living carpet across the plains, seen from above.

Is the Migration Worth Visiting Outside Crossing Season?

Absolutely. The Maasai Mara holds resident wildlife year-round — lion, leopard, cheetah, elephant and more — regardless of whether the migratory herds are present. Visiting outside July–October also means fewer vehicles at each sighting and often better rates. If witnessing a river crossing specifically is your priority, however, the July-to-October window in Kenya is when to plan your trip.

Planning Your Migration Safari

A migration-focused safari typically benefits from at least three to four full days in the Mara ecosystem, giving you multiple opportunities across different crossing points rather than resting your entire trip on a single day's luck.
